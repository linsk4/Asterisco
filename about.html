About us

who said this isn't for you*

We're building the thing we wanted to find: a way into sport and the outdoors that doesn't ask you to already be good at it.

What we are

a community, and a platform for leading

Asterisco* is a women-led community and umbrella organization that connects women through sport, movement, nature and shared experience.

We work on two levels at once. As a community, we create accessible, non-judgmental spaces where women can try new sports, spend time in nature, meet other women and learn from one another.

As an umbrella organization — meaning we support other people's initiatives rather than running everything ourselves — we back individual women who have an idea for an event or activity. We help her find partners, venues and a first real experience of leadership, and we handle the practical side around her.

Underneath both sits one conviction: movement and leadership are deeply connected, and both are still harder for women to reach freely than they should be.

The problem

the barriers are real, and they're rarely about fitness

For many women, access to sport, the outdoors and freedom of movement is still limited — by cultural, social, emotional and structural barriers rather than ability.

*

nobody to go with

Not knowing anyone who does it, and not wanting to turn up alone.

*

no idea how to start

No obvious entry point, no gear, and no one to ask the basic questions.

*

fear of judgment

The worry of being watched, or of not being “good enough” to belong there.

*

a persistent gap

Fewer women in sport, in the outdoors, and in the leadership roles around both.

These barriers rarely disappear on their own. They need spaces designed specifically to remove them: low-pressure, non-competitive, beginner-first, and built around relationships rather than performance.

Our vision

move without fear, and step into leading

We imagine a world in which women can freely inhabit their own bodies, move without fear, and step into leadership — of their own choices, their shared activities, and their communities. Sport becomes a tool for connection, confidence and belonging, not a performance to be judged.

Long term, we want Asterisco* to grow into a recognised international movement: a network of active local communities, connected globally, that keeps growing beyond any single event.

The umbrella model

two sides of the same empowerment

This is the structural idea that makes Asterisco* more than a series of events — and we treat both sides as one process, not two separate programmes.

*

leading — the event creators

Any woman with an idea can bring it to us: a sport she wants to introduce, a topic she wants to raise, an experience she wants to create.

Together we help her find partners, a venue and the practical pieces she needs. She takes care of the content, the promotion and the delivery. It's co-creation, but the woman with the idea is the one in the lead — deciding, organising and owning the outcome.

For many, it's a first real experience of leadership and responsibility. Consistently, both the women who lead and our own team describe it as the most empowering part of what we do.

bring us your idea

*

taking part — the community

The second layer is everyone who comes to try, connect and grow through the events themselves.

Barriers come down by offering access and space to try something new alongside other women — somewhere to exchange knowledge, attempt a sport for the first time, or simply meet people.

It's paired with reflection: every event is also a chance to raise a question, share what you know, or open a debate about something that matters.

see our events

leading an event and taking part in one build the same muscles: confidence, autonomy, connection, and a sense of ownership over your own body and choices.

Peer learning

women learning from women

A defining feature of Asterisco* is that expertise flows horizontally, between women, rather than top-down.

Events are consistently built around a woman who already knows something — how to fix a bike, how to climb, how to think about nutrition and movement, how to play padel — sharing that knowledge directly with others in an approachable, pressure-free setting.

It is not “come learn from us.” It is “come learn from each other, with us alongside you.”

The pattern repeats: someone in the community already has the skill, we create the structure for her to lead and share it, and the whole group's capability grows together.

Nature, bodies & resilience

the outdoors is central, not decorative

We treat time outside as more than a setting for activity — it's part of how the community builds resilience, individually and collectively.

freedom of movement

Outdoor activities and time in nature are core community-building practices. They offer freedom of movement, a shared sense of discovery, and a setting away from performance pressure and everyday expectations.

a parallel we take seriously

We draw a deliberate parallel between the way women's bodies are subject to control, judgment and pressure, and the way the natural world is subject to exploitation. Reconnecting women with nature — through movement and shared experience — becomes a source of care, solidarity and resilience for both.

room for real conversation

Every event is also a space for dialogue. Alongside the activity, we raise topics worth reflecting on together: the gender gap in sport and outdoor leadership, the relationship between the body and nature, self-love and accepting your own limits and desires, and what it actually takes for a woman to move from taking part to leading.

We're explicit that we don't claim to have the answers. The goal is to open honest conversations and support women in questioning their own routines and assumptions, together.

Movement outdoors plus open reflection on real topics — that combination is what turns a one-off activity into something that builds long-term awareness and community strength.

three women who found each other outdoors

What makes us different

a connective layer, not a competitor

We don't treat sport as an end goal or a route to competition. We treat it as a tool for connection, confidence, exploration and leadership.

We don't position ourselves against sports clubs, women's organizations or local community groups — we complement them. We're the layer that makes existing activities more accessible, and turns them into entry points for leadership, friendship and reflection.

And where most communities focus on a single sport, we're deliberately cross-sport: women move between climbing, cycling, running, padel, hiking and whatever comes next, learning from each other along the way.
